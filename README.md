## CBIO7501: The Art of Reproducible and Open Science
> Welcome to **CBIO7501** – a 2-credit hands-on course on **computational reproducibility and open science**

### Course description
This 2-credit course introduces the principles and practices of reproducible and open science in computational research. This course is designed to equip graduate students and postdocs with essential skills necessary for ensuring reproducibility in computational research. Students will gain hands-on experience with tools and techniques that ensure research transparency, reproducibility, and data integrity—skills now expected in academia, industry, and policy. Through real-world case studies and project-based learning, students will learn to manage data responsibly, use version control, create and containerize workfl ows, use literate programming and share research outputs following FAIR and open science standards. This course prepares graduate students to produce high-quality, shareable, and trusted scientifi c work aligned with best practices.
### Learning goals

This course aims to foster a culture of reproducibility, open science, open source and collaboration in research and provide the necessary tools and skills.

Through active engagement and completion of course activities, you will be able to:

- Explain the principles and importance of reproducible and open science in computational and biomedical research.
- Apply version control tools (e.g., Git/GitHub) to track and manage code changes in collaborative research projects.
- Create and use portable and reproducible computing environments (e.g. Pixi) and using containerization (e.g., Docker).
- Apply techniques for documenting and automating projects and workfl ows and generating reproducible results.
- Organize and document data and metadata in accordance with FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) principles.
- Publish and share research outputs openly using platforms for code, data, and preprints while adhering to licensing and ethical standards.
- Create a fully reproducible and open research project that demonstrates integration of all course concepts and tools.
